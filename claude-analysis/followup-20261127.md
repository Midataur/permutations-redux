# Follow-up tests, 27 November 2026 (evening)

Three tests after the snapshot survey (`snapshot-survey/README.md`). Every number is from one checkpoint per model;
`torn-bigger-13 (Feb 2025)` is the repo checkpoint used throughout the report, and `torn-bigger-13 (resumed)` is the
Spartan file saved after the November 2025 resume.

## 0. Test 16 on the elementary models (`elem_memorisation.py`, `elem_memorisation_results.json`)

Slots binned by hop count (40–49, 50–59, 60–69, 70–79, 80+), up to 300 per bin:
- training slots from 2M words of the committed boosted training data;
- validation slots from the run's validation file;
- fresh slots from the matched generator.

In all five models (`rerun-4`, `boosted-2`, `boosted-4`, `rerun-3`, `boosted-1`), training, validation and fresh
slots get the same accuracy (99.3–100%) and the same median margins, within about one logit, at every hop count. This
holds for `rerun-4` and `boosted-2`, whose wandb validation loss ends 3,000 and 700 times above their training loss.
So the elementary models do not know their own long chains better than fresh ones. `torn-bigger-13`'s pattern (Test 16,
report §3.13 and below) is specific to it, not a side effect of training on a static file in general.

## 1. Are the committed boosted files the reruns' training data? (`elem_train_val_check.py`, `elem_train_val_check_log.txt`)

Per-slot cross-entropy on 3,000 words of `train_data3.csv` (commit `fdefb8c`) against 3,000 validation words:

| model | training words | validation words |
|---|---|---|
| `rerun-4` | 4.4e-7 (no slot above 1e-3) | 1.6e-4 (about 5 of 48,000 slots above 1e-3) |
| `boosted-2` | 6.5e-8 | 2.8e-8 |
| `boosted-1` | 6.4e-9 | 7.1e-9 |

`rerun-4` fits the committed training words more tightly than its validation words, as it would if it trained on them.
The other two show no difference in this sample. Their wandb validation loss comes from a handful of wrong slots that
3,000 words rarely contain, so the test is weak. With the dataset size (16M words) and folder name also matching, I
take the committed files as the reruns' training data, but this is not proven.

## 2. Hops against distinct points (`hop_points_grid.py`, `hop_points_grid.json`, `hop_points_grid_log.txt`)

| 18 | 0.53 / 0.70 / 0.73 / 0.67 | 0.61 / 0.83 / 0.81 / 0.77 | 0.62 / 0.91 / 0.99 / 0.99 | |
| 20 | 0.54 / 0.50 / 0.56 / 0.51 | 0.65 / 0.68 / 0.64 / 0.59 | 0.67 / 0.78 / 0.94 / 0.93 | |
| 22 | 0.48 / 0.43 / 0.43 / 0.39 | 0.56 / 0.51 / 0.55 / 0.51 | 0.61 / 0.75 / 0.85 / 0.95 | |

**What it shows.**
- **`torn-bigger-13`'s limit is in hops, not points.** For chains through 5, 7 or 10 points the three columns agree within
  the noise at every $L$: near 0.99 to 14 hops, then 0.90–0.96 at 15–16, about 0.8 at 17, 0.7 at 18, 0.5 at 20 and 0.4
  at 22. Chains through only 3 points are worse at every $L$ from 13 on.
- **The fresh-data control fails in a different way, by points.** Through 10 points it is still at 0.93–0.95 at 20–22
  hops, but each step down in $s$ costs more: 0.85, 0.75 and about 0.6 at 22 hops for 7, 5 and 3 points. So the two
  models have qualitatively different weaknesses. The original cannot follow long chains whatever they visit; the
  control can, unless the chain keeps revisiting a few points, which natural words almost never contain.
- **The resume made `torn-bigger-13` slightly better, not different.** The resumed model is ahead in all 24 cells from
  15 to 22 hops, by 2 to 18 points. Most single cells are within two standard errors of no difference (about 6 points
  for a difference of two cells), but the direction never changes, and the shape of the decline is the same.
- **`g_plant10` is at 1.00 in all 40 cells.**

The natural-slot half of the grid (fresh slots split by distinct points, in the log) agrees, with fewer slots per cell.

## 3. The same slots through all four models (`run_memorisation.py`, `memorisation_results*.json`)

Test 16's slots: fresh natural slots, and the training slots of `torn-bigger-13`'s 8M-word file, which for every other
model are just more held-out words. Accuracy (median margin) on fresh slots:

| hops $h$ | Feb 2025 | resumed | `g_m10` (fresh data) | `g_plant10` (planted chains) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 13 | 0.989 (17.4) | 0.993 (17.4) | 0.990 (10.8) | 1.000 (14.6) |
| 14 | 0.976 (15.5) | 0.987 (15.8) | 0.989 (10.0) | 1.000 (14.5) |
| 15 | 0.943 (13.0) | 0.960 (14.1) | 0.985 (9.1) | 1.000 (14.6) |
| 16 | 0.868 (9.5) | 0.904 (11.0) | 0.978 (8.6) | 1.000 (14.6) |
| 17 | 0.719 (7.6), 32 slots | 0.750 (8.0) | 1.000 (8.1) | 1.000 (14.4) |
| on `torn-bigger-13`'s training slots, $h$ = 14 / 15 / 16 | 1.000 / 1.000 / 1.000 | 0.999 / 0.993 / 1.000 | 0.983 / 0.985 / 1.000 | 1.000 / 1.000 / 1.000 |

**What it shows.**
- **On fresh slots, `torn-bigger-13` is behind the control from 14 hops on:** by 1 point at 14, 4 at 15, 11 at 16 and
  about 28 at 17. On its own training slots it is at 100% throughout. This is the gap a static file *could* cost, but the
  control differs from the original in more than the data (report §3.14), so I do not attribute all of it to the file;
  the replication run will.
- **The margins tell the same story from another side.**
  - `torn-bigger-13` is very confident on short chains (median margin 18.8 at 12 hops) and its confidence drops steadily
    with $h$ (9.5 at 16).
  - The control is less confident everywhere but drops less (12.1 to 8.6).
  - `g_plant10`'s margin does not depend on $h$ at all (14.4–14.7 from 12 to 17 hops), nor on whether the slot comes
    from `torn-bigger-13`'s training file: whatever it does, it does the same at every chain length.
- **The resume did not erase the memorisation.** The resumed model is still at 99.3–100% on its training slots and
  a little lower on fresh ones (0.904 at 16 hops against 1.000).
